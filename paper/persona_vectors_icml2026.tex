\begin{abstract}
Persona vectors~\cite{chen2025persona} extract \emph{per-trait} linear
directions from a frozen LLM's residual stream for inference-time
steering.  Applied to \emph{per-user} identity on the LaMP
personalization benchmark~\cite{salemi2024lamp}, the picture is
\emph{variant-conditional}: the canonical template extraction yields
collapsed geometry, while a fact-based extraction recovers
user-distinguishable directions that the canonical operator
nonetheless fails to convert into a positive effect.  We isolate the
\textbf{operator bottleneck} on the fact variant -- where the per-user
direction is recoverable, but no single-layer additive operator at a
constant $\alpha$ amplifies it into a positive population-level
effect -- and show that the template variant fails earlier, at the
geometric step.
\textbf{(i)~Geometry: variant-conditional.}  We reject H1 for the
canonical template extraction (full-$n$ mean cosine $0.604$ on LaMP-2
and $0.811$ on LaMP-7 -- pairs share a dominant common direction).
We support H1 for fact-based extraction (LaMP-2
$\overline{\cos}{=}0.320$ at $n{=}323$; LaMP-7 $\overline{\cos}{=}0.272$
at $n{=}1497$; rank-at-90\,\% $18$/$110$).
\textbf{(ii)~The canonical additive operator underperforms zero-shot
and ICL at full $n$.}  On all $n{=}323$ LaMP-2 users: ZS $0.582$,
ICL-$K{=}6$ $0.582$, template steering $0.557$ ($\Delta{=}{-}0.025$),
fact steering $0.536$ ($\Delta{=}{-}0.046$).  On all $n{=}1497$
LaMP-7 users, ZS and ICL are statistically indistinguishable
($0.426$ vs $0.428$ ROUGE-L); steering is at parity.  Static
per-user proxies (alignment, magnitude, neighbour coherence)
\emph{do not} predict per-user $\Delta$ at full $n$ ($|r|{\le}0.14$).
\textbf{(iii)~Three operator-class alternatives stop the crash but
do not amplify.}  Across all four (task, variant) cells at full $n$
we test PCA-projection of population directions, ROME-style rank-1
weight edits, and $\cos$-gated token-selective routing.  The largest
gain anywhere is $+0.006$ ($\cos$-routing on LaMP-2 fact at
$\tau{=}0.25$).  No operator we test exceeds this.  On the fact variant, where extraction
quality is controlled, single-layer additive injection at constant
$\alpha$ -- and the family of operator-class generalizations we
explore -- is the limiting factor; the extractor is not.  Multi-layer
composition and learned per-user $\alpha$ schedules remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Personalization of LLMs.}
LLM personalization -- adapting a frozen pre-trained backbone to
individual users -- is a rapidly growing research area, motivated by
applications in personalized assistants, recommendation, and content
generation~\cite{salemi2024lamp,kumar2024longlamp}. The dominant
paradigm is parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) on per-user data:
prefix-tuning~\cite{li2021prefix}, prompt
tuning~\cite{lester2021prompt}, low-rank adaptation
(LoRA)~\cite{hu2022lora}, and per-user soft prompts produced by
trained adapters such as Q-Formers~\cite{li2023blip2,hebert2024persoma}.
An alternative is retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over the user's
history~\cite{salemi2024lamp,salemi2024optimization,
salemi2024comparing,shi2024collaborative,
amirizaniani2025multistep}, which conditions the model on retrieved
profile items at inference time. Both families improve over zero-shot
on standard personalization
benchmarks~\cite{salemi2024lamp,kumar2024longlamp}, but they share two
weaknesses. First, every user incurs additional trainable parameters
or context length. Second, the mechanism by which user identity is
delivered to the output distribution is opaque.

\paragraph{Persona vectors and representation engineering.}
A parallel line of work in mechanistic interpretability shows that
high-level concepts can be encoded as \emph{linear directions} in a
frozen LLM's residual stream~\cite{elhage2021framework,
park2024linear,zou2023repe}. Representation
engineering~\cite{zou2023repe}, contrastive activation
addition~\cite{rimsky2024caa}, and activation
addition~\cite{turner2023actadd,subramani2022extracting} extract such
directions from contrastive prompt pairs and steer the model at
inference time by adding $\alpha v$ to the residual stream. The same
construction has been used to mediate refusal in a single
direction~\cite{arditi2024refusal} and to control honesty and
sycophancy~\cite{rimsky2024caa,zou2023repe}. Most directly relevant,
\citet{chen2025persona} introduce \emph{persona vectors}: contrastive
system-prompt pairs yield a single direction whose addition or
subtraction reliably amplifies or suppresses the trait at inference
time on Llama- and Qwen-class backbones.

Recent operator-class generalizations relax this setup --
PERSONA~\cite{persona2026} predicts dynamic per-input $\alpha_i$;
SAE-RSV~\cite{saersv2025} substitutes sparse-autoencoder features;
PsPLUG~\cite{psplug2026} injects via training-time soft prompts --
and are precisely the generalizations our findings argue for.

\paragraph{The operator-bottleneck question.}
Do the same activations encode \emph{per-user identity} as a linear
direction \emph{and} can a single-layer additive hook deliver that
identity to the output distribution? Recent work
on attribute-style concepts (sentiment, refusal,
sycophancy)~\cite{park2024linear,arditi2024refusal,rimsky2024caa,zou2023repe}
shows that the geometric step and the operator step usually
\emph{succeed together}.  We show that for high-cardinality
identity-style concepts (LaMP user profiles) they \emph{decouple
on at least one variant}: with fact-based extraction the per-user
direction is recoverable, but constant-$\alpha$ additive injection
-- and three alternative operator classes we test
(PCA-projection, rank-1 ROME edit, $\cos$-gated routing) -- fail to
amplify it into a population gain.  The canonical template
extraction yields collapsed geometry instead, failing at the
geometric step alone.
We test this on Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B~\cite{qwen3technicalreport}
in non-thinking mode, matching the canonical persona-vector
setup~\cite{chen2025persona}.

\paragraph{Falsifiable hypotheses.} We pre-register two:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textbf{H1.}] \emph{Per-user persona vectors are linearly
  distinguishable in residual-stream geometry.} Falsified if the
  extracted vectors at the empirically best layer collapse onto a
  low-rank subspace ($\overline{\cos}\!\ge\!0.6$) that cannot be
  attributed to duplication artifacts.
\item[\textbf{H2.}] \emph{Inference-time additive steering at one
  layer is sufficient to deliver per-user identity to the output
  distribution.} Falsified if persona steering at the best layer and
  best $\alpha$ does not exceed zero-shot on average across users.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{H1 is rejected for template extraction
(full-$n$ $\overline{\cos}\!\ge\!0.6$) but supported for fact
extraction ($\overline{\cos}\!\le\!0.32$).  H2 is rejected on both
extractions and on three alternative operator classes -- combined
with H1 supported on fact, this isolates the operator bottleneck on
the fact variant; the template variant fails at the earlier
geometric step.}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Per-user persona-vector formulation and identification of a
  within-user duplication confound in LaMP-2 (\S\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:geometry}).
\item Refutation of the artifact-quality hypothesis: locally generated
  user-specific facts yield cleaner geometry
  (LaMP-7 $\overline{\cos}{:}0.41{\to}0.27$) but no downstream gain
  (\S\ref{sec:positive_control}).
\item A four-cell operator-class study (App.~\ref{app:operators})
  showing additive, PCA-projection, ROME rank-1, and $\cos$-routing
  all stay within $\Delta\!\in\![{-}0.046,{+}0.006]$ of zero-shot,
  with a Llama-3.1-8B cross-backbone replication.
\item Anonymized code, raw JSON, and figures at
  \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/persona-vectors-icml2026/}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Per-user artifacts.} For user $u$ with profile
$\{p_1,\dots,p_6\}$, we build $n_+{=}3$ positive prompts via the
template ``\emph{You are an author whose past work is exemplified by:}
$p_i$'' and a fixed pool of $n_-{=}3$ generic-baseline negative
prompts. Extraction questions are sampled from the LaMP train split
and shared across users. This is the simplest \emph{paper-faithful}
adaptation of the per-trait construction
of~\citet{chen2025persona}.

\paragraph{Vector extraction.} For each $(\text{system}_+,q)$ pair we
generate a response and pool the mean residual-stream activation at
layer $L$ over the response tokens; same for negatives. The per-user
vector is
\begin{equation}
v_u \;=\; \mathrm{mean}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{u,+}\bigr)
        - \mathrm{mean}\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{u,-}\bigr).
\label{eq:extraction}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Inference-time steering.} On the \emph{output} of decoder
layer $L$ (post-attention, post-MLP, pre-LN of $L{+}1$) we add
$\alpha v_u$ to every token: $h^{(L+1)}_t \!\leftarrow\!
h^{(L+1)}_t \!+\! \alpha v_u$. Canonical CAA placement~\cite{rimsky2024caa,
turner2023actadd}; alternative injection points not searched.

\paragraph{Setup.} Frozen Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B
(\texttt{fp16}, $4\!\times$NVIDIA V100), seed 42, Qwen3 thinking mode
disabled (\texttt{enable\_thinking=False} plus \texttt{/no\_think} in
the system prompt) per the model
recommendations~\cite{qwen3technicalreport}.

\section{Geometry: H1 rejected for template, supported for fact}
\label{sec:geometry}

\paragraph{The confound.} LaMP-2's dev split contains $1\,605$
examples but only $323$ \emph{unique} user profiles (mean $5.0$ test
items per user; LaMP-7 has $1\,497$ unique profiles in $1\,498$
examples). Naive analysis on the first $n{=}100$ samples treats
each row as a separate user and inflates inter-user similarity.

\paragraph{Template extraction: H1 rejected at full $n$.}
At face value Qwen3-8B/$L{=}13$ has mean cosine $0.749$ on LaMP-2
(naive duplicates; Tab.~\ref{tab:geometry}). Profile-hash dedup at
$n{=}30$ drops this to $0.329$ -- a small-sample artifact: at full
$n{=}323$ unique LaMP-2 users $\overline{\cos}{=}0.604$; at full
$n{=}1497$ LaMP-7 users $0.811$. Rank-at-90\,\% is $\ge 7$ (not
strictly rank-1 collapsed) but pairs share a dominant common
direction.  \textbf{We reject H1 for template extraction at scale.}

\paragraph{Fact extraction: H1 supported at full $n$.}
Locally generated user-specific facts yield markedly more
user-specific vectors: $\overline{\cos}{=}0.320$ (LaMP-2 $n{=}323$),
$0.272$ (LaMP-7 $n{=}1497$); rank-at-90\,\% $18$/$110$.
\textbf{H1 supported for fact extraction.}  Operator failure below
is observed on this geometry too -- decoupling extractor quality
from operator outcome.

Magnitude is comfortable for steering ($|v|/|h|{=}18.3\,\%$;
Fig.~\ref{fig:magnitude_app}), within the steering-effective
regime~\cite{rimsky2024caa,chen2025persona}.

\section{Steering: H2 fails on average}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Layer search.} Sweeping the middle 60\,\% of decoder layers
on LaMP-2 ($n{=}50$, stride 2) places the best layer at $L{=}13$ for
Qwen3-8B (36\,\% depth) and $L{=}10$ for Qwen3-14B (25\,\% depth);
deeper layers degrade accuracy (App.~\ref{app:layer_search},
Table~\ref{tab:layer_search_full}, Fig.~\ref{fig:layer_app}). The
optimal layer is earlier than typical hand-picked middle layers used
in prior steering work~\cite{rimsky2024caa,arditi2024refusal}.

\paragraph{Dose-response.} An $\alpha$-sweep on the best layer
($n{=}200$) yields a clean response curve: $\alpha{=}1$ peaks at
$+0.020$ accuracy over zero-shot; $\alpha{=}4$ collapses to $0.545$;
$\alpha{=}16$ destroys base capability ($0.000$;
App.~\ref{app:alpha}). Steering visibly perturbs the model -- the
operator is implemented and not silently broken.  The $+0.020$ peak
does not survive at $n{=}500$ (Extended evaluation below) and is
not load-bearing; reported only to localize the dose window.

\paragraph{Extraction questions saturate at $k{=}1$.} Increasing the
number of extraction questions $k\!\in\!\{1,3,5,10\}$ produces
\emph{bitwise-identical} predictions for $k{\ge}3$ (App.~\ref{app:nq}),
indicating that the vector saturates after a single question -- there
is no information to recover by adding more.  We attribute this to
LaMP profiles being short and topically homogeneous (six items per
user, mostly headlines); once the contrastive prompt-pair captures
the dominant ``act-as-this-author'' direction, additional questions
sample from the same low-entropy span.  This contrasts with the
canonical persona-vector setup~\cite{chen2025persona}, where global
character traits are exercised by $k{=}20$ diverse open-ended
questions; we expect saturation to set in later in that regime,
but do not test it.  The saturation we observe may therefore be to
a low ceiling rather than a sufficient signal: a richer profile
source (e.g.\ LongLaMP~\cite{kumar2024longlamp}) could raise it,
a confound we do not close.

\paragraph{Extended evaluation.} At $n{=}500$ on LaMP-2 the small
positive effect at $n{=}200$ does not survive: Qwen3-8B
$0.672 \to 0.660\,(\Delta{=}{-}0.012)$ and Qwen3-14B
$0.678 \to 0.666\,(\Delta{=}{-}0.012)$; both within the noise envelope
$\pm 0.022$ but identical in sign. We reject H2.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Positive control: artifacts are not the issue}
\label{sec:positive_control}

To test whether the gap is an artifact-quality problem we replace the
template positive prompt with five \emph{concrete user-specific facts}
generated locally by the same Qwen3-8B from the LaMP profile -- no
external API. At $n{=}100$ unique LaMP-2 users the fact-based variant
produces \emph{measurably more user-specific} vectors than the template:
mean off-diagonal cosine drops from $0.471$ to $0.347$ ($\Delta{=}{-}0.12$),
frac-cos$>\!0.8$ drops from $0.23$ to $0.02$, and rank-at-$90\%$ rises
from $9$ to $14$.  At $n{=}30$ unique LaMP-7 users the effect is
larger still ($\overline{\cos}$ drops from $0.413$ to $0.252$;
App.~\ref{app:positive_control}, Fig.~\ref{fig:positive_control_app}).
Yet downstream, fact-based steering performs \emph{worse} than
template on LaMP-2 ($0.433$ vs.\ $0.533$; $\Delta{=}{-}0.13$ from
zero-shot) and is within $0.005$ ROUGE-L of zero-shot on LaMP-7.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}  Fact-based positive prompts are longer and
yield larger vectors ($\|v_{\text{fact}}\|/\|v_{\text{tmpl}}\|
{=}1.24/1.88\times$ on LaMP-2/LaMP-7).  A natural ``overshoot''
hypothesis is that fact-based steering at $\alpha{=}1$ is past the
useful window because $\alpha\|v\|$ is too large.  We test this
directly by re-running additive steering with each $v_u$ rescaled
to the population median norm (App.~\ref{app:operators},
Op-norm-fixed): the crash persists almost unchanged
($\Delta_{\text{tmpl}}{=}{-}0.028$ at $\alpha{=}1$ vs. $-0.025$
unnormalized; $\Delta_{\text{fact}}{=}{-}0.043$ vs. $-0.046$),
\textbf{ruling out norm-overshoot as the sole cause}.  The
limitation is therefore in the constant-$\alpha$ direction-only
operator, not in vector magnitude.

\section{A user where steering works}
\label{sec:case_study}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_case_LaMP-2_user003_fact.pdf}
\caption{Single-user trajectory. (A) Top-$5$ token probabilities at
the answer position vs.\ $\alpha$. (B) Probability of the gold token
\texttt{politics} vs.\ $\alpha$. (C) Greedy prediction at each
$\alpha$ (gold green, wrong red). Phase transition between
$\alpha{=}0.50$ and $\alpha{=}0.75$.}
\label{fig:case_study}
\end{figure}

A representative user (id $3$ on LaMP-2) has a profile dominated by
women's voices, social-media humor and gender issues. Their test
input asks for the topical category of a politically-framed article;
zero-shot greedy decoding returns \texttt{education} with probability
$1.00$. Steering with their fact-based persona vector at $L{=}13$
produces a sharp phase transition (Fig.~\ref{fig:case_study}): between
$\alpha{=}0.50$ ($P(\texttt{ed.}){=}0.70$, $P(\texttt{pol.}){=}0.30$)
and $\alpha{=}0.75$ ($P(\texttt{pol.}){=}1.00$) the argmax flips to
the gold answer.

\paragraph{Is this cherry-picked?  A per-user $\alpha$-search across
all $n{=}323$ unique LaMP-2 users.}  We ran the full $\alpha$-grid
$\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1,1.25,1.5,2\}$ on every user and recorded the
minimum $\alpha$ at which the prediction first matches gold
(App.~\ref{app:alpha_search}, Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha_search_app}).  With
fact-based vectors, $29/323$ wrong-at-$\alpha{=}0$ users are flipped
to gold by some $\alpha$ (first-correct $\alpha\!\in\![0.25,2.0]$,
mean $0.66$, std $0.44$), while $48/323$ correct-at-$\alpha{=}0$
users are \emph{broken} by some $\alpha\!>\!0$.  Template steering
behaves similarly ($11$ flipped, $42$ broken).  The net effect of any
single global $\alpha$ is therefore $\mathrm{flipped}-\mathrm{broken}
{=}{-}19$ (fact) or ${-}31$ (template) -- broken consistently
outweighs flipped at every constant $\alpha$.  Individual users are
reachable; the canonical \emph{constant-$\alpha$} operator is not the
right delivery mechanism for a population-average gain.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Operator robustness is geometry-conditional.}  Across
four (task, variant) cells we test additive, PCA-projection,
rank-1 ROME, and $\cos$-routing (Tab.~\ref{tab:operators}).  Op1
recovers the $-0.025$ template crash but degrades fact ($-0.090$
at $k{=}1$); Op3 fires only on fact-extracted vectors and yields
the single largest gain ($+0.006$, LaMP-2 fact, $\tau{=}0.25$);
Op2 is uniformly stable ($|\Delta|\!\le\!0.003$) but never
amplifies.  \textbf{No operator crosses $\Delta{=}+0.006$} -- an
order of magnitude below trait-level
effects~\cite{chen2025persona,rimsky2024caa}.

\paragraph{ICL vs.\ steering.}  At matched per-user history, plain
ICL (six profile items as context) is consistently at least as
good as steering, but neither beats zero-shot at full $n$ (LaMP-2:
ZS${=}$ICL$_{K{=}6}{=}0.582$, steering $0.557/0.536$ tpl/fact;
LaMP-7: ZS $0.426$, ICL$_{K{=}3}$ $0.428$;
Tab.~\ref{tab:icl}, App.~\ref{app:icl_vs_steering}).  The $+0.02$
ICL gain at $n{=}100$ was a small-sample fluctuation.

\paragraph{No static proxy predicts per-user $\Delta$.}  Per-user
signal-quality proxies (alignment, magnitude, NN coherence)
correlate with $\Delta$ at $|r|\!\le\!0.39$ on $n{=}100$ but
attenuate to $|r|\!\le\!0.14$ at $n{=}323$
(App.~\ref{app:variance}): the early signal was small-sample noise.

\paragraph{Significance.}  Paired tests on per-user predictions
(Tab.~\ref{tab:icl}): LaMP-2 ($n{=}323$, McNemar exact) tpl
$p{=}0.17$, fact $p{=}0.08$, ICL $p\!\ge\!0.89$; LaMP-7 ($n{=}1497$,
bootstrap $B{=}10^4$) fact $\Delta{=}{-}0.006$, $p{=}0.017$ (only
cell with $p\!<\!0.05$); template and ICL straddle zero.
\textbf{The canonical operator never amplifies; on LaMP-7 fact it
significantly degrades.}

\paragraph{Weaknesses.}
(i)~Dedup LaMP-2 at $n{=}30$ is thin (full-$n{=}323$ resolves this).
(ii)~Primary backbone is Qwen3; Mistral-7B at $L{=}14$ reproduces
the crash sign but $L$ was not searched there -- a sign-preserving
lower bound, not a fair model comparison.
(iii)~$k{=}1$ extraction; $k{=}20$ with frontier-LLM
prompts~\cite{chen2025persona} untested ($k\!\le\!10$ does not
improve).
(iv)~Multi-layer composition and learned per-user $\alpha$ are out
of scope -- the bottleneck predicts these.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}\label{sec:conclusion}
With fact-based extraction, per-user identity is a recoverable
linear direction, but no single-layer constant-$\alpha$ operator
we test delivers it (largest gain $+0.006$).  \textbf{Where
extraction is controlled, the operator -- not the extractor -- is
the bottleneck; template extraction fails earlier at geometry.}

extracted persona vectors $\{v_u\}$ and correlate them with the
per-user $\Delta$ROUGE-L (steered minus zero-shot).
\begin{itemize}
\item P1: $\cos(v_u, \bar v)$ where $\bar v$ is the population mean.
\item P2: $\|v_u\|/\overline{\|v\|}$ -- effective perturbation at
  constant $\alpha$.
\item P3: mean cosine to the $k{=}5$ nearest-neighbour user vectors.
\end{itemize}

\section{Per-user $\alpha$-search (full)}
\label{app:alpha_search}
We sweep $\alpha\!\in\!\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1,1.25,1.5,2\}$ for every
user.  At full $n{=}323$ unique LaMP-2 users:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Template variant:} $188$ correct at $\alpha{=}0$, of which
$42$ are \emph{broken} by some $\alpha\!>\!0$; $11$ wrong-at-$\alpha{=}0$
users are flipped to correct (first-correct $\alpha\!\in\![0.25, 2.0]$,
mean $0.886$, std $0.444$); $124$ are never correct.  Net effect of
any constant $\alpha$: $11-42{=}-31$ users.
\item \textbf{Fact variant:} $188$ correct at $\alpha{=}0$, of which
$48$ are \emph{broken}; $29$ wrong-at-$\alpha{=}0$ users are flipped to
correct (first-correct $\alpha\!\in\![0.25,2.0]$, mean $0.664$,
std $0.437$); $106$ are never correct.  Net effect of any constant
$\alpha$: $29-48{=}-19$ users.
\end{itemize}
Broken consistently outweighs flipped at every constant $\alpha$, on
both variants; this is the mechanism behind the population-average
$\Delta\!\le\!0$ of \S\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Alternative steering operators}
\label{app:operators}
The body shows that single-layer additive steering at constant $\alpha$
\emph{degrades} accuracy on LaMP-2 ($-0.025$ at $\alpha{=}1$) while
geometry is well-formed.  We test three operator-class modifications,
holding the extracted vectors $v_u$, layer $L{=}13$, and dataset
fixed.  Each is a strict generalization of the additive baseline and
reduces to it under specific parameter settings.

\paragraph{Op1 -- projection-nuisance.}  Compute the top-$k$ PCA basis
$B\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}$ over the population of all $v_u$ and
steer with the residual:
$\tilde v_u = v_u - B^{\!\top}\! B\, v_u$.  Top-$k$ PCs capture
\emph{variance shared across users} -- typically a dominant
``act-as-author'' template direction; removing them isolates
user-specific residual.

\paragraph{Op2 -- rank-1 weight edit (ROME-style).}  Instead of
adding $\alpha v_u$ at a single forward pass, we apply a persistent
rank-1 update to the MLP \texttt{down\_proj} at layer $L$:
$W_{\text{down}}\!\leftarrow\!W_{\text{down}} + (s_u/\|k^{\!*}\|^2)
\,(v_{\bar u}\!\otimes\!k^{\!*})$, where $v_{\bar u}$ is the mean
user vector, $k^{\!*}$ is the mean intermediate-dim activation at
the last prompt token, and $s_u\!=\!\alpha (v_u\!\cdot\!v_{\bar u})/
\|v_{\bar u}\|^2$ scales by per-user alignment with the population
mean.  The edit persists through all subsequent layers (in contrast
to additive, which is mixed away by attention/MLP
downstream)~\cite{meng2022rome}.

\paragraph{Op3 -- token-selective routing.}  Apply additive steering
only at positions whose pre-injection hidden state has high cosine
with $v_u$:
$h_l[t]\!\mathrel{+}=\!\alpha v_u\,\mathbb{1}\!\bigl[\cos(h_l[t],
v_u)\!>\!\tau\bigr]$.  At $\tau\!=\!-\infty$ this recovers the
additive baseline; at $\tau\!\to\!1$ no token is steered.

\paragraph{Findings.}
(i)~\emph{Op1 helps only when there is a population direction to
remove.}  On LaMP-2 template, where additive steering crashes by
$-0.025$, removing the top-$k\!=\!5$ PCs recovers the deficit
entirely and yields $+0.003$ at $\alpha{=}0.5$ (removed variance
$\approx 30\,\%$).  On LaMP-2 fact, LaMP-7 template, and LaMP-7
fact -- where the population direction is already absent or weak
(App.~\ref{app:positive_control}) -- Op1 either degrades (LaMP-2
fact: $-0.016$ at $\alpha{=}0.5,k{=}5$, down to $-0.090$ at
$k{=}1$) or stays inside $\pm0.002$ of zero-shot (LaMP-7 template/fact
at $n{=}1497$).  Op1's effect is therefore not a generic recipe but a
geometry-conditional one.
(ii)~\emph{Persistent rank-1 weight edits are uniformly stable.}
Op2 stays within $0.003$ of zero-shot on every task variant we test:
LaMP-2 template ($-0.003$ at $\alpha{=}2$), LaMP-2 fact ($0.000$),
LaMP-7 template at $n{=}1497$ ($+0.002$ at $\alpha{=}2$), LaMP-7
fact at $n{=}1497$ ($+0.001$ at $\alpha{=}0.5$).  The per-user
alignment scaling $s_u$ keeps the edit small for users misaligned
with $v_{\bar u}$, which avoids the additive crash at the cost of
amplification.
(iii)~\emph{Token-selective routing fires only on fact geometry.}
On template-extracted vectors the cosine of any token with $v_u$ is
typically $\!<\!0.1$; no $\tau\!\in\!\{0.25,\dots,1.0\}$ ever fires
the gate (Op3 collapses to zero-shot).  On \emph{fact-extracted}
LaMP-2 vectors the gate fires for a non-trivial fraction of tokens
at $\tau{=}0.25$, and the resulting selective steering produces
the largest gain we find anywhere: $+0.006$ over ZS, stable across
$\alpha\!\in\![0.5,2]$.  This is a small but real effect tied to
the fact-extraction protocol producing higher token-direction
overlap than the template variant.
(iv)~\emph{No operator we tested converts geometry into a
$>\!+0.006$ gain.}  The gap between $\Delta\!\approx\!0$ and a
meaningful per-user effect is not closed by any single-layer
operator-class change.  The remaining levers are multi-layer
composition, learned per-user $\alpha$ schedules, and richer
extraction protocols ($k{>}1$ extraction questions; multi-layer
extraction).

\paragraph{Implications for the body.}  Tab.~\ref{tab:operators}
strengthens the conclusion of \S\ref{sec:discussion}: limitation
(iv) (multi-layer / per-token / projection-out) was previously
listed as out-of-scope; we have now tested two of three
generalizations directly.  A shared-template projection
(Op1, $k{=}5$) erases the additive deficit and is the single
operator change we identified that does not hurt accuracy on
LaMP-2; a persistent weight edit (Op2) is comparably benign.
Neither converts the per-user geometric signal into a meaningful
downstream gain.

\paragraph{Norm-fixed control (reviewer Q1).}

explained purely by vector magnitudes ($\|v_{\text{fact}}\|/\|v_{\text{tmpl}}\|
{=}1.24$ on LaMP-2).  We re-run additive steering with each $v_u$
rescaled to the population median norm $\|v\|_{\text{med}}$ (Op-norm-fixed
